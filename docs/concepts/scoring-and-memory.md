# Scoring and Memory

**Traces are facts. Scores are policy. The persistent memory of every measurement ever taken is the measurement archive — the central data interface.**

A trace records what the pipeline did (query, prediction, ground truth, node rankings, timeouts). A score judges *over* a trace; the answer changes with what you're optimizing for. Traces are written once and never edited; scores are a view produced by applying the active policy on demand.

**The archive's shape — the fold, its two derived views, its two retrieval keys, and the content-addressing that makes it cross-cycle** — owned by [`../developer/README.md`](../developer/README.md#4-cross-run-memory). One row there is one **measurement**, `(sample × config → outcome)`; this page owns only what a *score* over that row means.

## Rescore-on-load

A trace can be judged under many policies, so no score is stored as the trace's own truth — **the archive holds none at all.** Its write seam strips every grade a row carries (`fitness`, `objective`, `unscored`: `domain/scoring.py::GRADE_KEYS`), so a banked row is what was measured — the answer, tokens, latency, the backend's observations, a judge's banked verdict — and nothing a formula made of it. A row in MEMORY may carry grades for the round's own use; the seam, not the caller, keeps them off disk.

Every read grades under a scorer it names, through ONE derivation: facts → graded cells (`application/scoring/formula/rescore.py::rescore_results`) → the population (`is_graded`, § Which rows a reading counts) → folds → readings. Fresh samples, cache hits, trial reloads, the δ ruler, the indexes, cross-campaign reads and every mask all take that step, so the `fitness` / `objective` you read is always the reading formula's view, and rows banked under one formula read under another exactly as a fresh run under it would. **A lens is a scorer, not a formula over aggregates**: `score:F` is the campaign's scoring block with `per_cell` set to `F` — the override a fork applying it runs under — so "what would this campaign read under `F`" and "run it under `F`" are one number wherever `F` is nonlinear, which the length charge below is ([`../operations/mask-projection.md`](../operations/mask-projection.md)). **The bench headline is one more such reading.** A bench pass banks facts (`domain/bench.py::BenchPass`: which individual, the archive run, the rows it sent, whether it stopped, the grader it ran under), and `application/runner/bench.py::read_bench` is the one derivation from them to a `BenchScore` — for the run that graded the pass and every later reader alike. Every stored `BenchScore` is a cache of one such reading, stamped with its `scorer_id`; a reader under another grader reads the passes again. A formula that cannot be evaluated at all (`ScoringFormulaError`, a contract bug) halts the run; it is never written into a row. What a formula CHANGE does to a running cycle is owned by [`../operations/persistence-and-state.md`](../operations/persistence-and-state.md) § Changing the composite formula — fork, never swap.

---

One of the downstream consequences is the system keeps **two costs**, and they answer different questions:

- **The bill** — money that actually left the account. Cache hits contribute nothing to it. This is the headline, and it is what the spend budget caps; a published or head-to-head figure also carries the raw tokens and the price table's date and never omits the optimization spend, which the bill already meters ([`../research/external-constraints.md`](../research/external-constraints.md) § Cost reporting). It has to stay this way: billing a replay would halt a run that cost nothing to make.
- **The incurred cost** — what the search would cost to run against a cold cache, with cache hits priced from the tokens they recorded (the cached payloads carry them, so nothing is estimated) — each cell once per campaign, since a cold run re-reading its own cell (a parent re-scored each round, a resume, a fork) replays it free too. This is what a *measurement of a candidate* has to divide by — and a bench lift divides by the SEARCH's share alone (`domain/spend.py::SEARCH_KINDS`), never the bench pass that graded it, and by nothing where the search carries tokens no rate priced. **A controlled arm's ceiling caps this one instead** (`CeilingMeter` `search_incurred`): arms of one head-to-head may replay each other's cells, so a ceiling on the bill would hand whichever arrived second a larger search for the same money.

On a cold cache the two are equal; they part once the archive is deep enough for an arm to replay cells another paid for.

### Two caches, and the word only belongs to one

The costs above turn on OUR cache. A provider keeps its own, and the two are named apart everywhere:

- **A replay** — our content-addressed archive answered, so the call never reached a provider and nothing was billed. It is a **boolean**: `TokenUsageRecord.cached`, the `📖` on a sample line, `replayed` on an optimizer line. This is the one the two costs above are about.
- **A prefix-cache read** — a provider *did* serve the call and billed part of its input at a discount, because our prompt's leading bytes matched one it had already processed. It is a **token count**, a SUBSET of the input: `TokenUsageRecord.cache_read_tokens` / `cache_write_tokens`, `SpendBucket.cache_read_tokens`. Every surface renders it through one reading — `domain/spend.py::TokenAccount.cache_share`, whose required `replayed` kwarg is what stops a replay being shown a discount it never bought.

The rule is that **one word cannot mean both**: `cached` is the replay, never the count. `account_spend` drops a record from the spend roll-up whenever `cached` is truthy, so a provider's count filed under that name hides paid calls from the budget ceiling.

Two consequences worth knowing before reading any capture number. The provider cache is **per-replica and warms by repetition** — a prefix sent once warms one machine in a fleet, so capture climbs over a run rather than appearing on the second call; measured, a repeated prompt missed, then held 92–99% from roughly the fourth call on. And it is **why the optimizer prompt orders its fields the way it does** ([`opt_search_point.py::OptimizerPromptTemplate.RENDER_ORDER`](../../promptpotter/domain/opt_search_point.py)): everything volatile renders last, because a single per-round value early in the prompt voids the discount on every byte behind it.


## Deprecated samples

**Which advisories and response shapes derive a fatal or infra code** — owned by [`../developer/self-healing-internals.md`](../developer/self-healing-internals.md), whose rule table is the only version of it; the word itself is [`../../promptpotter/domain/CLAUDE.md`](../../promptpotter/domain/CLAUDE.md)'s. What belongs here is what a *score* over such a row means: a deprecated result is evicted from cache and re-measured, **but it still counts** — a refusal or a truncation is what the prompt produced, so it is the miss the formula grades (§ Which rows a reading counts). The trace itself stays in `measurements/` either way.

## Which rows a reading counts

**A reading's population is the population that was sent.** One predicate decides it for every number an optimizer elects on and the bench headline alike — `domain/scoring.py::is_graded`, whose list form is `classification.py::scoreable_rows`. Three kinds of row leave: a **provider or machine fault** (`shared/errors.py::ERROR_IS_CHARGED` says which categories), a cell that ran to its end with **nothing to grade** (`UNSCOREABLE`), and a row the formula **cannot grade** (`unscored`). Everything the configuration caused stays: a refusal, a truncation or an empty answer is graded by the formula, and a charged error — a cut cell (`HALTED`), a rejected config, an unexplained failure — is a miss at 0.0 wherever a label defines one. On a labelless bank a formula alone defines a verdict, so a charged error carries none there. A denominator that drops the prompt's own failures pays it accuracy for failing on exactly the cells it could not answer — and **a bench pass that ends with more rows lacking a verdict than its split's `tolerance` reads nothing**, since a headline over a population other than the one sent is not the bench score.

## Decision replay + fork

Optimizer choices derive from scored numbers, so they are replayable at all — that is this page's thesis paying out. On resume the optimizer rescores under the current scorer and re-runs each recorded decision against the rescored view: match → the round stands, mismatch → halt at the divergence point. What happens next is a fork, and its mechanics are [`../operations/persistence-and-state.md`](../operations/persistence-and-state.md) § Recovery.

Decision records are two-tier, on the same facts-vs-policy line: **replayable** (which candidate won, the parameters that gated the choice) vs **archival** (full LLM outputs, diagnostic context — never read by replay).

## Composite — recorded, not gating

Round-winner selection compares candidates on difficulty-adjusted ability (θ on the cycle's fixed δ ruler — subset-invariant, so candidates scored on different adaptive subsets stay comparable). **θ is fit on the composite, not on accuracy**: each cell carries what it was WORTH (`objective`) beside whether it was RIGHT (`fitness`), so a campaign that prices latency, cost or a provider's flakiness elects on that price rather than merely displaying it. Declare one under `campaign.yaml::scoring.per_cell`; absent, the two are the same number and nothing changes. **A miss is charged its cost too.** A composite that scales correctness by a cost factor scores every miss 0.0 whatever it spent, so θ could not tell a runaway miss from a cheap one. Each cell's `objective` is therefore `(1 − s)·per_cell(row) + s·per_cell(row with fitness 1)`, with `s` = `formula/compiler.py::MISS_COST_SHARE`: a solved cell scores exactly its composite, and a miss keeps `s` of what the same cell would have scored solved. The share is part of the grading function, so it enters the scorer id — as do the campaign's judges, whose banked terms the formula reads. A charged error is a miss at the floor, since it recorded no cost to keep a share of, while an episode that ran out of turns or its own clock and was graded is a scored miss, so its cost counts (§ Which rows a reading counts). **A length charge is BOUNDED, so accuracy dominates it.** Every shipped token-charged template declares `fitness * ((1 − λ) + λ · B / max(B, tokens))` with `λ = 0.08` and `B` the origin's median tokens: 1.0 up to `B`, falling monotonically toward `1 − λ` and never below it. An unbounded factor lets a long enough hit score under a cheap miss, and lets length overturn any accuracy gap. With `s = 0.2` the guarantee is exact. Per cell, a hit scores in `[0.92, 1]` and a miss in `[0.184, 0.2]`, so no miss beats a hit. Per arm, the mean composite lies in `[(1 − λ)·E(a), E(a)]`, with `E(a) = s + (1 − s)·a` at accuracy `a`, so arm A beats arm B at any lengths once `(1 − λ)·E(a_A) > E(a_B)`. That holds for every accuracy gap above `λ / (1 − s)` = 10pp. It puts 100% at any length (≥ 0.92) above 50% at none (≤ 0.6), and a 2× gap is safe wherever the lower arm is above `sλ / ((1 − s)(1 − 2λ))` ≈ 2.4%. Inside 10pp, length decides only against a much longer prompt: from an arm at `B` and 42% accuracy, +1pp is worth up to +23% tokens, +2pp up to +57%, +3pp up to 2.2×, +5pp up to 7.6×. At equal accuracy the shorter prompt always wins. **Accuracy** displays alongside as the subset-relative correctness rate, which is what makes a win that came with hidden costs visible as a gap between the two.

θ is the standard IRT/CAT fix: a small statistical model that **structurally** removes the per-round sample-set drift — when the adaptive picker hands each candidate a different subset, raw accuracy is no longer comparable, but ability is. **The model, and when a dataset graduates from 1PL to 2PL** — owned by [`../methods/verdict-resolution.md`](../methods/verdict-resolution.md) § The model — Rasch θ/δ.

Why two forks share one archive without duplicating a measurement — content-addressing, in [`../operations/persistence-and-state.md`](../operations/persistence-and-state.md) § The primitive.
